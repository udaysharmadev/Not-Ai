# Multilingual scope

Read this reference when the draft is not in English. The short version:
the editorial contract travels, the measurements mostly do not.

## What transfers unchanged

The parts of the skill that are about responsibility, not language:

- the writing contract (purpose, audience, genre, register, protected
  content, unknowns);
- the source ledger and the no-invention rule;
- the revision receipt;
- the diagnose shape and the intervention levels.

A bracketed gap is honest in any language. An invented detail is not, in
any language.

## What does not transfer

Everything measured is English-specific until a language appendix says
otherwise:

- the tier vocabulary lists and their inflections;
- the participial-opener patterns;
- the nominalization suffixes;
- the contraction check and its per-genre floors;
- the Flesch-Kincaid grade bands;
- the sentence splitter's abbreviation list.

Running these over non-English text produces findings that look
authoritative and mean nothing. When the draft is not English, say which
checks were skipped and why, and weigh the contract and the author's own
reference text more heavily than any figure.

## Contributing a language appendix

A language appendix lives at `reference/lang-<code>.md` and contains only
what has been checked against real prose in that language:

1. the sentence boundaries that actually occur (abbreviations,
   particles, script-specific punctuation);
2. the function-word list the voice comparison should count;
3. the stock-phrase clusters documented for model output in that
   language, with sources, and the same keep-when-precise rule;
4. one worked before/after pair showing the appendix changing a real
   decision.

Do not machine-translate the English lists. Calqued patterns are the
fastest way to manufacture false findings in another language.
