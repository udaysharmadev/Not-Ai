In this semester project, our team designed and evaluated a low-cost air-quality logging station for the civil engineering workshop at IIT Roorkee. Furthermore, the primary objective of the investigation was to compare particulate matter readings from three Plantower PMS5003 optical sensors against the reference gravimetric sampler located 12 meters away on the roof. By utilizing a Raspberry Pi 4B and an SQLite database, we recorded PM2.5 and PM10 concentrations at 60-second intervals between 14 January 2026 and 28 February 2026. Moreover, it is important to note that winter fog events in late January caused relative humidity to exceed 85% on nineteen mornings, which significantly inflated the optical scattering counts relative to the dry filter reference.

Additionally, when we inspected the raw time-series logs, we observed that Sensor B exhibited a systematic positive offset of 11.2 ug/m3 whenever ambient relative humidity rose above 80%. To address this limitation, we implemented a two-parameter Kohler-style hygroscopic correction using readings from a co-located SHT31 temperature and humidity probe. After applying the humidity correction across the 45-day dataset, the root-mean-square error (RMSE) between the PMS5003 triad average and the daily gravimetric reference dropped from 24.8 ug/m3 to 9.6 ug/m3, while the Pearson correlation improved from r = 0.78 to r = 0.93.

However, the correction model did not resolve rapid spikes caused by diesel generator testing in the courtyard every Tuesday morning between 09:00 and 09:30. During those 30-minute plumes, the optical sensors saturated above 320 ug/m3 because the fan intake rate was too slow to clear coarse soot particles without clogging the inner baffle. Furthermore, we found that cleaning the intake chamber with dry compressed air every 14 days prevented baseline drift during February. In conclusion, the project demonstrates that optical particulate sensors can provide useful workshop exposure logs if paired with local humidity compensation and a biweekly maintenance schedule, although they cannot replace reference gravimetric filters for regulatory compliance.
