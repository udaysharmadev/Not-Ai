To isolate sentence segmentation from lexical choice, we constructed a balanced corpus of 360 English passages stratified across six registers: undergraduate lab reports, peer-reviewed methods sections, technical documentation, workplace correspondence, reflective essays, and short social posts. Each register stratum contained 30 human-authored passages written before November 2022 and 30 passages generated by instruction-tuned language models on matched topic prompts. Passages in the short stratum ran between 90 and 140 words (mean = 108.4, SD = 11.7), whereas passages in the long stratum ran between 310 and 480 words (mean = 364.2, SD = 38.5) to satisfy the 300-word minimum required by institutional screening tools such as Turnitin (2024).

For each source passage, we applied six deterministic, word-preserving punctuation transformations while holding the case-folded alphanumeric token sequence strictly invariant. We verified word-sequence invariance using character opcode diffs and SHA-256 token-stream digests. Before perturbing clause or sentence punctuation, we masked non-prosodic periods and commas inside decimal numbers (for example, 3.14 or 1,000), timestamps (09:30 a.m.), uniform resource locators, parenthetical year citations, and standard abbreviations (such as Dr., Fig., or U.S.) so that factual literals remained untouched. We then measured how each perturbation changed rule-based sentence splitters, dependency-parsed clause counts, and byte-pair encoding (BPE) tokens near sentence boundaries.

Across the short stratum, removing a single internal sentence period reduced the detected sentence count by 12.5% and raised the sample coefficient of variation (CV) of sentence length from a mean of 0.382 to 0.541 (paired median difference = +0.148, IQR = 0.092). In the long stratum, the same single-stop deletion shifted sentence-length CV by only +0.039 (IQR = 0.028), showing that short passages are disproportionately sensitive to single-boundary edits. However, blinded human raters (k = 4) judged 91.7% of period-deletion variants in formal registers to be ungrammatical run-on sentences, confirming that boundary deletion damages prose clarity even when it inflates statistical variance metrics.
