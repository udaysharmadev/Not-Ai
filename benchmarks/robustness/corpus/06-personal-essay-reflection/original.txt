Looking back at my first winter in Shimla, it is worth noting that the power cuts taught me more about patience than any textbook ever could. Every evening around 6:15 p.m., the voltage would dip until the filament bulb above the kitchen table glowed a dull orange, and then the room went dark. Furthermore, my grandmother never complained when the heater died; instead, she pulled the brass kangri closer, poured salted butter tea from the thermos, and made me recite my history dates from memory. In conclusion, those quiet two-hour blackouts fostered an enduring sense of solace and resilience that still shapes how I work when deadlines get noisy.
