Transformer-based text classifiers often lose calibration under out-of-domain and surface perturbations (Dugan et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024). We test whether subword boundary shifts from punctuation edits alter predicted probabilities across four open-weight encoder detectors (n = 2,400 passages). Deleting sentence-final periods or inserting tight ellipses shifts mean predicted AI probability by 11.4 percentage points (95% CI [8.9, 13.8], p < 0.001), largely because fused sentences double measured sentence-length variance. Surface-sensitive classifiers may therefore confuse typographic segmentation changes with syntactic burstiness unless inputs are normalized before inference.
