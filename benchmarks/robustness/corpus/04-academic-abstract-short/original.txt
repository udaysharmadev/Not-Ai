Recent advances in transformer-based classifiers have underscored the importance of evaluating out-of-domain robustness in automated text screening (Dugan et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024). In this study, we examine whether subword boundary shifts induced by punctuation perturbations alter class probability calibration across four open-weight encoder detectors (n = 2,400 passages). Results indicate that deleting sentence-final periods or inserting tight ellipses shifts mean predicted AI probability by 11.4 percentage points (95% CI [8.9, 13.8], p < 0.001), primarily by fusing adjacent sentences and doubling measured sentence-length variance. These findings suggest that surface-sensitive classifiers may conflate typographic segmentation changes with syntactic burstiness unless inputs are normalized prior to inference.
